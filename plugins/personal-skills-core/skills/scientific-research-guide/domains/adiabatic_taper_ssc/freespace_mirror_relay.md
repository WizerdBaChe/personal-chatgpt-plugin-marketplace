# Sub-profile: Free-Space Mirror Relay Coupling (in-slab etched curved micromirrors) — under Adiabatic Taper / Spot-Size Conversion

> Parent domain: `adiabatic_taper_ssc.md`
> Branch axis: **method** — an alternative mechanism for the same fiber↔chip mode-conversion problem the parent owns.
> Scope: fiber-to-chip coupling where the mode relay is performed by **etched curved mirrors inside a planar slab** rather than by an adiabatic taper — glass/silica photonic interposers, in-plane micromirror collimators, slab free-propagation regions used as beam relays. Stops at: AWG/Echelle **as wavelength demultiplexers** (sibling territory — their loss budget carries dispersion-specific terms this branch does not need); the taper itself (parent).
> Inherits from parent: Nodes 1–3 (theory / tools / toolchain) unless overridden below.

> **Decision point.** When the user describes relaying fiber light across a substrate with curved mirrors, confirm before advising: "Is your goal (A) **mode-size conversion** toward a smaller waveguide, (B) **path folding / routing** with the mode size preserved, or (C) **out-of-plane redirection** onto a grating coupler?" These have different dominant error terms, and only (A) requires the mirror to mode-match.
> **SSLD ruling (2026-08-30, T13):** that project is **(B) + (C) at magnification ≈ 1 — NOT (A)**. Do not import (A)-class selling points (Xu2023's 22:1, Moreno-Pozas's 570:1 conversion) into it; at M≈1 a single mirror already closes the link optically (see Node 4) and the second mirror buys only landing-point freedom.

## 4. Branch-Specific Design Rules and Fitting Methods

| Method / rule | Applicable conditions | Common error | Correct approach | Source [Key] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mirror sizing criterion `f / z_R < 5` | Spherical micromirror collimating a Gaussian fiber mode in-plane | ⚠️ Choosing the focal length from the desired spot size at the mirror, then finding alignment sensitivity unmanageable | Set `f` from the criterion FIRST, let the spot size follow. **Single-source criterion** — Sabry 2013 and 2017 are the same team/device family; no independent replication found in two search waves. Flag it as such | [Sabry2017] |
| Off-axis astigmatism — WHICH geometry it applies to (corrected 2026-08-30) | Any curved facet used off-axis | ⚠️ Applying `Δf = (R/2)(1/cosθ − cosθ)` to a **vertical sidewall mirror inside a slab** — it is a *cylindrical* mirror; the sagittal direction is index-guided by the slab and has no focus to split. Companion error: citing echelle θ_in = 46–62° as proof large single-mirror folds work (see Node 6) | The criterion is real but belongs to **out-of-plane 3D beams** (Sabry's bi-axial mirrors, with focal-length matching at θ_inc = 0°/45°/60°) — and to any vertical out-coupling segment. In-slab mirror designs by three independent teams (Janz, Moreno-Pozas, Melati) are pure-2D with no astigmatism term. For the slab case the binding fold-angle limits are **cone-TIR and the GH phase gradient** (rows below). Whether a circular arc suffices vs a true parabola in 2D is **unproven** — spherical aberration/coma at the design NA not yet computed | [Sabry2017], [Janz2025], [MorenoPozas2025], [Melati2019], [T13-audit] |
| **A parabolic sidewall can do the whole in-plane mode conversion** | In-plane conversion between very different mode sizes | ⚠️ Assuming a mirror can only relay, and that a taper must finish the job | Measured: a parabolic-mirror collimator converts a **450 nm waveguide to a 10 µm waveguide** (~22:1) at **≈0.15 dB over 100 nm bandwidth**, footprint ~**20 × 32 µm²** — the paper states this is *much shorter than the linear taper* needed for the same loss. The "mirror must be paired with a taper" intuition holds for **spherical** mirrors; aspheric changes the answer **in-plane only** | [Xu2023] |
| Gaussian invariant — what it does and does not forbid | Any mirror-relay mode-conversion claim | ⚠️ Using `w₀·θ = λ/(πn)` to argue mirrors cannot mode-convert at all | The invariant constrains the **beam parameter product**, not the achievable conversion ratio. It sets the NA the optic must deliver; an aspheric facet can deliver it. What the invariant genuinely forbids is beating it with a *lossless in-plane* element in the **out-of-plane** direction, where a sidewall mirror has no power at all | [calc], [Xu2023] |
| Roughness→loss: which model governs | Specifying a mirror surface figure | ⚠️ Quoting one σ threshold as a physical constant | Two defensible models disagree by >3×: scalar TIS `(4πσcosθ/λ)²` (power scattered out) gives σ≈26 nm for 0.1 dB at 45°, λ=1550; a modal-coupling model gives σ<0.06λ (≈93 nm) for 95% coupling. State which mechanism is assumed to dominate — no source found that analyses both together | [Sabry2017], [calc] |
| Roughness spec must carry a correlation length | Writing any mirror surface spec | ⚠️ Specifying σ_RMS alone | The modal threshold is Lc-dependent: σ < 0.04λ / 0.06λ / 0.1λ for Lc = 5λ / 10λ / 20λ. **A σ-only spec is incomplete** — AFM must report Lc too, or the spec cannot be adjudicated after the fact | [Sabry2017] |
| Scalar TIS validity guard | Estimating scatter loss from roughness | ⚠️ Extrapolating TIS into σ/λ > 0.05, where it saturates and prints confident nonsense | Refuse to output a number outside the smooth-surface limit; say "the instrument cannot determine this" and move to vector scattering or measurement | [calc] |
| Cone-TIR bound on the beam-turn angle | Uncoated TIR sidewall receiving a DIVERGING beam | ⚠️ Checking TIR for the chief ray only | TIR must hold across the whole aperture: `θ_chief ≥ θ_c + θ_div/4` (Moreno-Pozas eq. 5), with θ_div their >95%-power full cone `2.35λ/(2w·n)` ≈ 1.85× the classical 1/e² value. Glass/air with an SMF-28 mode: beam-turn cap **α ≤ 86°** — a 90° fold already violates their eq. (6). Positive control: the rule reproduces Janz's measured 33–58° aperture-incidence range from their ±26° divergence | [MorenoPozas2025], [Janz2025], [T13-audit] |
| GH phase gradient: stay far from the critical angle | Curved TIR wall (local incidence varies across the aperture) | ⚠️ Designing just above θ_c "because TIR is binary" | The TIR phase (Janz eq. 4, p-pol for slab TE) has dφ/dθ diverging at θ_c; across a ±1.8° beam the wavefront error is 0.177λ at θ_inc=45° vs 0.025λ at 60° (7×). Advisory: **θ_inc ≥ 55°** (beam turn α ≤ 70°). No quantitative curved-wall GH model exists — Janz: "will require extensive numerical modelling" | [Janz2025], [T13-audit] |
| Collimated-arm length is a PAID-FOR parameter | Two-mirror relay with a "collimated" middle section | ⚠️ Treating L_slab as free routing distance; assuming a symmetric relay can park the waist at mid-span | A focal length `f` cannot place a waist farther than `f + f²/(2z_R)`; past ~the confocal parameter the beam outgrows the second mirror (Janz: "loss becomes significant when the relay length exceeds the confocal parameters"). Consequences: mid-span waist may be geometrically UNREACHABLE (SSLD: 883 µm max vs 1500 µm wanted); M2 needs its own R + aperture sized to the beam it actually receives, or the arm must shrink | [Janz2025], [T13-audit], [calc] |
| Two mirrors = layout freedom, not optical necessity (at M≈1) | Fiber↔GC relay with magnification ≈ 1 | ⚠️ Assuming the collimate+refocus pair is optically required | At M≈1 a SINGLE mirror at object=image distance closes the link (unit-magnification Gaussian imaging; SSLD check: f = 266 µm reproduces the fiber waist exactly) and removes one focal-length co-design gap. The second mirror buys free landing-point placement — a LAYOUT purchase; say so explicitly when justifying the Z topology | [T13-audit] |
| Collimation mirror curvature is **2·R_wf**, not R_wf | Sizing a mirror to collimate a Gaussian at distance L from the waist | ⚠️ Setting R_mirror equal to the wavefront ROC — that mirror retro-focuses instead (caught live by a waist-at-mirror assert, 2026-08-30) | Collimation needs f = R_wf(L) = L·(1+(z_R/L)²), i.e. **R = 2·R_wf**. The far-field shortcut R = 2L understates R by the (z_R/L)² factor — 6% at L/z_R = 4. Assert "waist sits at the mirror" at build time; the wrong formula passes every eyeball check | [T18-audit], [calc] |
| Out-of-plane single mirror: the channel PITCH caps the object arm | Per-channel mirrors in an array at pitch p, one mirror doing fold+focus | ⚠️ Optimizing the imaging condition first, then discovering the mirror aperture no longer fits the channel | 3w(L1) ≤ (p − margins) bounds L1 from above; below the 1:1-imaging L1 the relay is forced to M > 1 with a mode-mismatch floor η = (2w₁w₂/(w₁²+w₂²))². SSLD @127 µm: L1 ≤ 581 µm ⇒ **−0.50 dB inherent**, shrinking as p grows — an architecture-level pitch↔loss coupling, not a tuning error | [T18-audit] |
| Expanded beam ⇔ standoff identity | Any lensed/expanded-beam vertical coupling stage | ⚠️ Assuming a bottom-surface lens can focus a fat beam across a flip-chip-scale gap | Focal throw ≈ π·w_lens·w_target/λ. Focusing 25 µm → 4.6 µm at 1310 nm needs ~270 µm of standoff; a 50 µm chip gap only supports w_lens ≈ w_target (no expansion at all — the strong-focus root prints w ≈ 0.4 µm and −14 dB). Pluggable-class standoff is *implied by the physics*, which is why detachable expanded-beam connectors are sockets | [T18-audit], [calc] |
| V-groove opening sets a channel-pitch floor | Fiber array in V-grooves at pitch p, fiber seated by wall tangency at center depth h | ⚠️ Treating groove pitch as free layout; checking only fiber diameter vs pitch | Opening `2a = 2(r + h·cosβ)/sinβ` (wall inclination β from horizontal, tangent to the fiber); minimum over β is 2r at vertical walls — and h is NOT free: it is locked to the waveguide depth, which is locked to the flip-side diffraction path. Desmet-class groove (β=63.2°, h=43.5 µm): opening 184 µm ⇒ 127 µm pitch geometrically dead (web −57 µm), 250 µm leaves 66 µm web. Escape routes are U-grooves (different tolerance mechanism) or in-glass fan-out, not "etch smaller grooves" | [T22-audit], [Desmet2021] |
| Polished end-facet fold: the angle is a DERIVED quantity | Angled-polished substrate facet folding a horizontal guided beam down to a GC | ⚠️ Copying a published facet angle (e.g. 41.5°) across wavelength / GC spec / exit medium | `m = (90° − θ_glass)/2`, incidence on the facet = 90° − m, θ_glass = asin(sin θ_air/n). 41.5° is the 1550 nm + 7°-in-silica solution; an 8°-in-air GC at 1310 gives 42.24°. Polish sensitivity dθ_air/dm ≈ 2n·cosθ_g/cosθ_air ≈ 2.9°/° — spec the jig angle with the GC's angular 1-dB budget, not "as the paper did" | [T22-audit], [Desmet2021] |
| Mounting orientation is an optical variable (flip = path-length lever) | Interposer with an embedded waveguide at depth d in a substrate of thickness T | ⚠️ Treating flip vs face-up as a packaging convenience | The mirror→GC glass path is d (flipped, waveguide side down) vs T−d (face-up): 43.5 vs 456.5 µm on a 500 µm substrate. Quantified @1310: 1.4–1.6 dB vs 11.4 dB mismatch — the flip IS the design. Assert the orientation in the model; the wrong one renders identically in a top view | [Desmet2021], [T22-audit] |
| Index-matching adhesive is two-sided: per-face sign | Any glued fiber/interposer assembly with both TIR facets and transmission faces | ⚠️ One global "use index glue" or "avoid glue" ruling | On a TRANSMISSION exit face the glue buys back BOTH Fresnel (0.147 → 0.003 dB) and diffraction (divergence slows by n: ~0.5 dB at a 50 µm gap); on a TIR facet the same glue is a kill (index-matched wetting → ~28 dB, see the Node 6 fill row). Draw adhesive keep-out zones per face; capillary wicking toward the polished facet is the failure path during interposer-to-PIC gluing | [T22-audit] |

## 6. Branch-Specific Assumption Pitfalls (core payload)

| Pitfall | Trigger condition | How to recognize it | Correct approach | Source [Key] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The design is drawn in-plane; the out-of-plane dimension is never addressed** | User presents a top-view sketch of a slab with curved sidewall mirrors | Every figure is a plan view; slab thickness is unstated | An etched sidewall mirror focuses **in-plane only**. Vertical confinement is a separate, harder problem: a slab thick enough (~10 µm) to accept a fiber mode is multimode unless Δn ≲ 0.002 (IOX-glass / doped-silica PLC territory, **not** SiN). A single-mode SiN slab (0.4–0.8 µm) costs 8–11 dB in vertical mismatch alone. Force the three-way choice before any layout work | [calc] |
| **"We'll make the slab out of SiN" — thickness is a hard material wall** | Any proposal for a SiN layer thicker than ~1 µm, especially one sized to a fiber mode | Layer stack lists a µm-scale or 10 µm-scale stoichiometric SiN film | Record crack-free thickness is **1.35 µm** (photonic Damascene: pre-patterning + LPCVD + CMP + anneal); bilayer stress relief reaches 740 nm; un-engineered films crack at **250–400 nm**. Intrinsic tensile stress >800 MPa accumulates ~linearly with thickness — **material physics, not immature process**. Redirect to fs-laser-written glass (30 µm core, **0.02–0.037 dB/cm**), SiN-in-glass low-confinement bonding (150 nm SiN, 0.62 dB/cm), or thin SiN + a separate vertical converter | [Pfeiffer2016], [McNulty2025], [Fernandez2025], [Tsvetkov2026] |
| **LPCVD assumed available on any glass** | SiN deposition specified on a glass substrate | Process flow says LPCVD without naming the glass | LPCVD runs 770–800 °C. Eagle-XG-class display glass has strain 669 °C / anneal 722 °C → **practically ruled out** (large-panel warpage). Fused silica (strain 893 °C) survives it. PECVD (200–400 °C) is fine on both. **The glass choice and the deposition choice are one decision, not two** | [W3] |
| **N–H absorption ignored when picking the band** | Low-temperature PECVD SiN specified for C-band | Band chosen for PIC compatibility with no loss check | The N–H overtone peaks near **1520 nm at 6.8 dB/cm**; even 25 nm off-peak still costs 2.3 dB/cm. But this is a *recipe* problem, not a band veto: NH₃-free PECVD at 350 °C measures <1 dB/cm at 1310 nm and 1.5 dB/cm at 1550 nm. Ask which recipe before conceding the band | [W3] |
| **Index-filling a mirror gap silently kills TIR** | User proposes filling the free-space region with a high-index medium "so the light doesn't spread" | Fill index > substrate index, while the mirror was assumed uncoated | Low→high index has **no** critical angle. Filling converts a free TIR mirror into one that MUST be metallized. On **glass the margin is already critical-zone with air**: n_air/n_silica = 0.69 ≈ the SOI-TM case (0.70) that Moreno-Pozas say "strongly constrains the divergence angle to be less than 1 degree" — their fix was REMOVING cladding to reach 0.5. Any fill on glass kills the architecture, not just one mirror | [calc], [MorenoPozas2025] |
| **"Air scatters the light" as the stated mechanism** | User explains beam spreading as scattering by air/vacuum | Proposed remedy is evacuation, or filling "to stop the scattering" | The mechanism is **diffractive divergence**, not scattering (z_R = πw₀²n/λ ≈ 55 µm for SMF-28 at 1550 nm in air). Index filling helps, but only linearly in n. Naming it correctly changes the remedy | [calc], [Sabry2013] |
| **Mirror roughness treated as the dominant loss term** | Project plan allocates most effort to mirror polishing | Loss budget not written down, or written only for the mirrors | In a full budget the grating coupler and polarization mismatch dominate; four mirrors at σ≈30 nm contribute ~10%. Roughness is the **highest-variance** term, not the median-case dominant one. Correct framing: "control the variance, then the GC governs" | [calc], [Djogo2025] |
| **Polarization omitted from the budget entirely** | Any fiber→interposer→GC link budget | The itemized budget has no PDL row | A published system of this class carries **~3 dB of polarization mismatch — its second-largest term**. A conventional GC is strongly TE-selective. Omitting the row does not omit the loss | [Djogo2025] |
| **Amorphous substrate assumed to have a self-limiting groove angle** | V-groove specified on glass/fused silica | Design cites 54.74° or expects an etch stop | Glass has no crystallographic stop. However the angle is **not** the dominant error: at the stationary width `W = 2r·cos ψ`, `dh/dψ = 0` and the seated fiber height becomes `h = r·sin ψ` (102.1 µm for a 125 µm fiber at ψ=35.26°). Redirect scrutiny to groove-width precision and **groove-to-waveguide overlay** | [calc] |
| **Prior art misclassified by application instead of by mechanism** | User cites a glass-interposer paper as "the same as ours" | The comparison is drawn from the abstract | Check the optical mechanism, not the application. The closest-looking system (fs-laser glass, micromirrors, vertical GC coupling, 3.2/5.0 dB) uses **flat elliptical TIR turning mirrors in a fully guided 3-D waveguide circuit** — not curved mirrors in a slab FPR. Same application, different physics, different failure modes | [Djogo2025] |
| **Echelle demux loss budget borrowed wholesale** | User plans to reuse published echelle numbers | Budget cites 1.4–5 dB as "what mirrors cost" | Borrow the *engineering toolbox* (Rowland/TSP aberration correction, reflector selection, loss-attribution patterns — consistent across SOI, Si₃N₄ and glass, across five institutions), not the *number*. A complete demux carries wavelength-selectivity overhead this branch does not need. The comparable sub-class is "single curved mirror + slab FPR" | [W2] |
| **Free-space optics drawn floating above the substrate** (caught by the user, 2026-08-30) | Any schematic/3D of an on-substrate free-space relay | Mirrors or lenses hover in air; only the fiber is a *placed* part | Everything except placed parts must be hosted by substrate material (etched, buried, molded or bonded) — and hosting **changes the physics**: the beam runs in glass (z_R ×n, the cone-TIR table applies to every fold, the entry face adds Fresnel/etalon terms), and "raise one side" of a monolith can only be built as recess-the-fiber-side, fully-buried (fs-written), or a bonded cap. Encode the invariant as a machine assert with the floating layout as its negative control. A back-fold Z in the vertical plane puts M1 at θ_inc ≈ 30° < θ_c → coating mandatory; the forward 45°+37° cascade keeps both mirrors TIR-capable but is no longer the sketched Z | [T18-audit] |
| **Echelle ANGLES transplanted to single-mirror folds** (caught live 2026-08-30) | Any claim of the form "devices run θ_in = 46–62°, so our fold angle is literature-proven" | The cited angles come from echelle demultiplexers | In an echelle those angles are measured to the **grating pole normal** of a near-Littrow layout: Melati's input/output waveguides sit **2–2.2 µm apart** at the slab edge — the beam turn is ~**178°**, and each blazed tooth carries its own local normal besides. Grating-equation angles NEVER convert to a single-mirror beam-turn angle; fold-angle limits must come from first principles (cone-TIR, GH gradient, fiber clearance — Node 4). This exact transplant survived two review passes in SSLD before full text killed it | [Melati2019], [T13-audit] |
| **Figure dimensions treated as self-consistent data** | Rebuilding geometry from a paper's dimensioned micrograph | Two readouts of the same figure imply different angles (63.2° caption vs 60.1° from the 72/125 slope readback) | Figure readouts carry ±1–2 µm / ±3°-class rounding. Rebuild the ONE consistent solution from a physical constraint (here: wall tangency to the fiber) + the caption nominal, then use a figure-level SUM (72+40+72 = 184) as the audit anchor with a formula that reproduces it; tag residuals `[lit-fig rounding]`. Never average the contradictory readouts | [T22-audit], [Desmet2021] |
| **A replicated process flow audited against memory instead of its own citation** | A teammate/vendor flow that names ONE source paper | The flow "looks right" step-by-step but nobody re-read the source | The cited source IS the audit ruler: re-read it full-text and diff per step with page-anchored quotes. The three gap classes found in practice: dropped whole steps (fixation/singulation/metrology), re-ordered steps that silently destroy the source's core advantage (simultaneous groove+waveguide inscription = the mutual-alignment selling point), and dropped acceptance values (jig angle, Ra targets). A flow can copy every recipe and still lose the mechanism | [T22-audit], [Desmet2021] |
